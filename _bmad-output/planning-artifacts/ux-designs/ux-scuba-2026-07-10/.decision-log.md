## D3 — Data honesty win (to confirm)
Josie's reframe maps to data we HAVE, fixing gaps in the shipped version:
- Fish biodiversity → iNaturalist species richness (601/601 locations) — vs the
  old "Fish life" which used biomass we could not grade → was "not surveyed".
- Bleaching risk → NOAA DHW / alert level (already computed).

## D4 — Biodiversity source: iNaturalist REJECTED
Josie: iNaturalist species counts reflect LOGGING RATE (observer effort), not
true biodiversity — popular/accessible sites score higher regardless of the
reef. Dishonest as a biodiversity signal. Removed from consideration.

Honest alternative found: Reef Life Survey `speciesRichness` = mean fish species
per standard M1 transect (500 m²) — effort-standardized, so it IS true richness.
BUT coverage is only 57 of ~601 locations (~9%); richness range 4.8–88.5,
median ~35. Everywhere else = honestly "not surveyed".

Open design choice (A vs B):
- A: biodiversity is a fixed row, "not surveyed" on ~91% of reefs.
- B (leaning): biodiversity row only appears where RLS data exists; card shows
  3 signals normally, 4 where surveyed. No dead row.

Open science item: grading Rich/Moderate/Sparse needs a "vs a healthy reef of
its kind" benchmark (reef-type relative) — NOT an absolute cutoff. Do not invent.

## D5 — Verdict scales CONFIRMED
- Coral health: Improving · Stable · Declining
- Fish biodiversity: Rich · Moderate · Sparse
- Bleaching risk: Low · Elevated · High
- Fishing: Protected · Limited · Heavily fished

## D6 — Biodiversity data-source landscape (beyond RLS)
Honest effort-standardized but REGIONAL: REEF (Caribbean/US/ETP, species per
survey), AGRRA (Caribbean), Reef Check (global method but ~20 indicator species
only, not full richness). Broad-coverage aggregators (GBIF, OBIS) share
iNaturalist's logging-rate bias — no fix. Modeled (AquaMaps/FishBase) = EXPECTED
richness, not observed — but good candidate for the "healthy reef of its kind"
benchmark. Conclusion: no honest GLOBAL observed-richness source; best = stack
RLS + REEF + AGRRA (>57 reefs, still not global), AquaMaps as benchmark.
